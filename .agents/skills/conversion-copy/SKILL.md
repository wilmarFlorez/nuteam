# Conversion Copy

Use this skill when writing or reviewing headlines, sections, CTAs, form labels, error messages, metadata, and other customer-facing copy for NuTeam.

## Context

- Audience: Spanish-speaking business decision-makers evaluating operational automation.
- Offer: NuTeam designs AI agents that execute operational processes and work with existing teams.
- Primary conversion: qualified contact form submission.
- Tone: direct, precise, credible, operational, and human. Avoid hype and vague futurism.

## Copy process

1. Identify the audience, job to be done, operational pain, desired outcome, proof, and next step.
2. State the user benefit before describing the technology.
3. Make the promise specific enough to understand and modest enough to prove.
4. Use concrete verbs and familiar Spanish; remove filler, jargon, and repeated claims.
5. Give every section one job: orient, explain, build confidence, or convert.
6. Finish with a clear CTA that says what happens next.

## NuTeam rules

- Prefer "agentes de IA para procesos operativos" over unexplained AI terminology.
- Explain human collaboration, review, limits, and handoffs where relevant.
- Never invent customer names, metrics, savings, guarantees, capabilities, or integrations.
- Label examples and hypotheses as such; request evidence before turning them into claims.
- Keep form labels specific and low-friction. Do not ask for information that is not used.
- Preserve the project's Spanish language and established dark, technical visual voice.
- Avoid manipulative urgency, fear, absolute promises, and phrases such as "revoluciona tu negocio".
- Write accessible text: descriptive link labels, meaningful headings, readable error messages, and no information conveyed only by punctuation or styling.

## Review checklist

- Can the target customer understand the offer in the first screen?
- Does the headline describe an outcome rather than only a technology?
- Does each section answer a real objection or question?
- Are claims verifiable and supported by available evidence?
- Is the CTA consistent throughout the page and clear about the next step?
- Are Spanish grammar, accents, capitalization, and terminology consistent?
- Does the copy remain clear on mobile and when read by a screen reader?
- Does the metadata describe the actual page without keyword stuffing?

## Deliverables

When proposing copy, provide:

- The recommended version first.
- The user's problem or objection it addresses.
- Any claim that needs evidence before publication.
- One concise alternative only when it represents a meaningful tradeoff.
